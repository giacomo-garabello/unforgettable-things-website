Privacy policy

Unforgettable Things for Android. Effective 6 November 2026.

The short version. No ads, no analytics, no tracking. Without sync, everything you add stays on your phone, and it only leaves when you export or share something yourself.

Sync is optional and paid. When you turn it on, your catalogue and photos are encrypted on your phone before they leave, with a key the server never gets. So I can't read them, and neither can Cloudflare, which hosts the server. The server does know your email address, your name, who's in your household and whether you subscribe.

Who's responsible

Unforgettable Things is made by Giacomo Garabello, an independent developer based in Italy. I'm the data controller for what this policy describes, under the EU's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). "I" and "me" below means the developer.

This policy covers the Android app, its sync server at sync.unforgettablethings.app, and this website. You can reach me at feedback@unforgettablethings.app.

Photos

You add photos with your camera or from your gallery. The app only gets the photos you pick and never browses the rest of your gallery.

Before a photo is saved, the app removes its metadata (EXIF, XMP and IPTC), which can include the GPS location, the camera model and the time it was taken. Only the orientation is kept, so the photo shows the right way up. The same is done to every photo a backup brings in. Photos are also resized to at most 1600 pixels on the longest side to save space.

Permissions

  • Notifications. Asked the first time you add a date to a pin, so the app can notify you at 9:00 on the day and on any early alerts. Notifications are scheduled on your phone, not sent from a server. With App lock on, they don't show the pin's name or where it is. You can turn them off in Android's settings, which Account › Settings › Notifications opens for you.
  • Run at startup. Lets the app put your scheduled reminders back after the phone restarts. It does nothing else.
  • Fingerprint or face. For the optional App lock. Android's own prompt checks your fingerprint, face, PIN, pattern or password, and tells the app only whether it worked. The app never gets your fingerprint or face data. While App lock is on, the app is also hidden from the recent apps screen.
  • Internet. Used only by sync, by Google sign-in and by the subscription. With sync off, the app doesn't connect to any server.
  • Google Play billing. Lets you buy the sync subscription through Google Play.
  • Camera and photos. The app doesn't ask for these. "Take a photo" opens your phone's camera app, and "Choose from gallery" uses Android's photo picker. Either way, only the photo you choose is handed to the app.

What isn't collected

The app has no analytics, no advertising ID, no ad networks and no automatic crash reporting. It doesn't read your location, contacts or anything else on your phone. Nothing about you is sold, used for advertising or used to build a profile of you.

Backups, reports and sharing

  • Export makes a .zip backup or a PDF report, of everything or of one room, photo or close-up. The app hands it to whatever you pick in the share sheet, such as Files, Google Drive, email or a chat app. From there, that service's privacy policy applies.
  • A .zip backup can be protected with a password (AES-256). The password isn't stored anywhere, so a forgotten one can't be recovered. Without one, the .zip isn't encrypted. PDF reports can't have a password. Keep both somewhere you trust.
  • Import backup adds a .zip to what's on your phone, or replaces everything with it. Either way, it stays on your phone.
  • Share where it is sends one picture the app draws on the spot: the photos from the room down to that thing, with only the next pin marked on each. It carries no metadata and no other pin's name.
  • Google's cloud backup is turned off for this app, so your data isn't copied to Google's servers automatically. If you move to a new phone with Android's phone-to-phone transfer (Android 12 and later), your data can be copied directly between the two phones. Sync turns itself off on the new phone until you sign in again, because the key that locks your sign-in stays on the old one.

Cloud sync

Sync is optional and paid. It's off until you turn it on in Account › Sync. It keeps the phones of a household on one catalogue, photos included, and keeps a copy safe if a phone is lost or broken.

Signing in with Google

Turning on sync opens Google's sign-in. Google gives the app your Google account's email address, name, profile picture and a sign-in token. The app sends the token to the sync server once; the server checks it with Google and keeps only your Google account ID and your email address. Your name becomes your name in the app, so your household can see who changed what. Your profile picture is downloaded once and stays on your phone.

Sync also asks for access to the app's own hidden folder in your Google Drive. That's where it keeps a copy of your household's key, so a new phone can open your data with nothing to type or remember. The app can't see any of your other Drive files, and you can't browse that folder either.

What's encrypted, and who holds the key

Your catalogue (rooms, pins, labels, dates, notes and the names attached to changes) and every photo are compressed and then encrypted on your phone with AES-256-GCM before they're uploaded. Each household has its own random 256-bit key. It stays on your household's phones and in each member's own Google Drive, and it never reaches the server. Even the photos' file names are replaced by codes, so the server can't tell when they were taken.

So Cloudflare holds the files but not the key, and Google holds the key but not the files. I can't read your catalogue or see your photos, and the server never opens, reads or logs them.

The server's own log records only which kind of request came in, its result and its size: no IP addresses, account IDs, file names or contents. Like any server on the internet, it sees your phone's IP address while it answers a request, but it doesn't store it. Cloudflare's network may process IP addresses to deliver traffic and protect against attacks.

The server and its database run on Cloudflare. The database and the encrypted files are stored in the European Union.

Households and invites

Everyone in a household shares one catalogue, so each member sees everything in it, and the names of the others. Only the person paying sees the subscription's details.

An invite is a link that works once, within 3 days. It carries the household's key after the # sign, a part of a link that browsers and apps never send to a server, so neither the sync server nor this website sees it. Anyone who opens the link in time can join and read the household's catalogue, so only send it to the person you mean to invite.

The owner can remove members. When someone leaves or is removed, what they shared stays with the household, and the copy of the catalogue on their own phones stays with them.

The subscription

Sync is bought through Google Play, monthly or yearly, and one member's subscription covers the whole household. Google takes the payment, so I never see your card or bank details.

When you buy, the app gives Google Play your sync account's random ID (not your email), so the purchase can be matched to your account. The server checks each purchase with Google Play and acknowledges it, and Google Play tells the server about renewals, cancellations and refunds as they happen. Google also gives me sales reports and an order record for each purchase, such as the order number, plan, price, date and country, which I keep as long as tax law requires. Google handles payments under its own privacy policy.

Deleting your data

  • On your phone. Delete any room, photo or pin, and empty Recently deleted to remove things straight away. Uninstalling the app removes everything it stored on that phone. Backups you exported stay wherever you saved them until you delete them there.
  • Your sync account. In the app, open Account, tap the pencil next to your name and choose Delete sync account. Without the app, use the deletion page. What's deleted and what's kept has the details. Your catalogue stays on your phones.
  • A household. Leave household removes you from it. When the last member leaves, the household and all its files are deleted.
  • The key in your Drive. Deleting your sync account from the app deletes it too. To remove it yourself: Google Drive on the web, Settings, Manage apps, Unforgettable Things, Options, Delete hidden app data. While sync is on, the app puts it back the next time it checks, because it's what lets a new phone in.

Feedback, bug reports and email

Send feedback and Report a bug, under Account › About and on the app's error screens, start an email in your phone's mail app. You see everything in it, and can change or delete any of it, before you send. You can send it with or without usage data: the app's version, your phone's model and Android version, the app's language, how many rooms, pins and deleted things you have and how much space the photos take, and the error log. Usage data never includes names, notes or photos. Send feedback can also open the app's Google Play page, where reviews are covered by Google's privacy policy.

When you write to feedback@unforgettablethings.app or bugs@unforgettablethings.app, I receive your email address, your name if your email app includes it, and whatever you write or attach. Cloudflare forwards these emails to my inbox. Emails to bugs@ are also saved as issues in the app's private code repository on GitHub, so I can track and fix each problem. Only I can see them. I use what you send only to answer you and improve the app.

Your rights

You can ask me to:

  • tell you what I hold about you, and give you a copy;
  • correct it;
  • delete it;
  • stop or limit using it, or object to how I use it;
  • give you what you gave me in a format you can take elsewhere. For your catalogue, Export backup does this in the app: a .zip with your photos and an open JSON file.

Email feedback@unforgettablethings.app and I'll answer within one month. To protect your account, I may ask you to write from the email address you use for sync. Because your catalogue and photos are encrypted, I can't give you a readable copy of them from the server: the app on your phone is the way to them.

You can also complain to the data protection authority where you live or work, or to Italy's Garante per la protezione dei dati personali.

Children

The app is for a general audience and isn't aimed at children. Sync needs a Google account and a Google Play subscription: if you're under 14, ask a parent before turning it on. If you think a child has a sync account without a parent's consent, email me and I'll delete it.
